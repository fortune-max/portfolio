## The old way

Every quarter a VC firm writes its investors a short report on each company in the portfolio: revenue, burn, runway, headcount, whatever else that company tracks, and a few lines on how it's going. The raw material is whatever the founders send, usually a deck, sometimes a PDF, now and then a spreadsheet with creative formatting.

An analyst read each one, found the numbers, compared them with last quarter and wrote it up. That took about four hours per company, for every company, every quarter.

## What changed

Since Q3 2025 a pipeline writes the first draft of almost every report, and the analyst checks it instead of writing it. A check takes 10–15 minutes, so the team gets back roughly 100 hours a quarter.

The part I cared about most was trust. An LLM will read a deck and give you a confident summary, and in a report to investors a confident wrong number is worse than a missing one. So every figure in a draft has to say where it came from (slide 7, page 3), and the analyst checks it against that.

## How it works

<ReportPipeline />

For each company, the pipeline looks at which KPIs it reported last quarter and builds a structured-output schema for this run, with one field per KPI. Each field holds the value, the unit, the period and the source. I use Pydantic's `create_model` for that, so a marketplace gets asked about GMV, a SaaS company about ARR, and nobody gets asked about things they don't track.

Gemini fills the schema from the new deck. The result is checked for types and for a source on every value, and written into our reporting workspace in Notion as a draft.

## Caveats

It drafts; it doesn't publish. An analyst is still the author of every report, and nothing goes to investors without that review. Founders who change their KPIs between quarters still need a human to decide what to do, and decks that are mostly images need more checking.

## Stack

Python and Django with Celery workers, Pydantic for the per-company schemas, Gemini for extraction (we moved over from an earlier OpenAI and LangChain version), and a small Notion client with retry on rate limits for writing the drafts.
